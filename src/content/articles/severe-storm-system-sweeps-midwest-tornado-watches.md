A volatile storm system is barreling across the central Plains on Saturday, triggering tornado watches for parts of **Kansas, Nebraska, Iowa, and Missouri** and severe thunderstorm warnings for more than 4 million residents in its path.

Forecasters said the system was organizing into a fast-moving line capable of producing wind gusts above 75 mph, hail the size of golf balls, and a few embedded tornadoes through the evening hours.

## Timing and impacts

The most dangerous window was expected between 4 p.m. and 10 p.m. local time as the line surges eastward toward the Mississippi River. Major cities in the risk zone include Topeka, Kansas City, Des Moines, and Omaha.

- **Tornado watch** in effect until 9 p.m. for much of eastern Kansas and western Missouri.
- **Wind gusts** of 60–75 mph could down trees and power lines.
- **Flash flooding** possible in urban corridors where storms train over the same areas.

## Safety guidance

The National Weather Service urged residents to have multiple ways to receive warnings, including a weather radio and phone alerts, and to identify an interior, lowest-level room before storms arrive.

> "These line-type systems can spin up brief tornadoes very quickly with little lead time," a forecaster said. "If you're under a watch, treat it as a dress rehearsal — know exactly where you'll go."

Power companies pre-positioned repair crews, and several school districts canceled evening activities. The system is forecast to weaken as it crosses the Appalachians early next week.
