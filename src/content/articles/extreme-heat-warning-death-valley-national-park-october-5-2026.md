The National Weather Service issued a Extreme Heat Warning Monday for Death Valley National Park, where dangerously hot conditions with high temperatures near 110 degrees and overnight lows only getting down to around 85 degrees. This will yield at least major Heat Risk, impacting anyone without adequate cooling and hydration, according to the agency's alert.


## What the warning says
The NWS alert states that dangerously hot conditions with high temperatures near 110 degrees and overnight lows only getting down to around 85 degrees. This will yield at least major Heat Risk, impacting anyone without adequate cooling and hydration.

The NWS notes potential impacts: heat-related illnesses increase significantly during extreme heat events.


## Areas affected
The alert covers Death Valley National Park.

The NWS said the warning applies to Elevations in Death Valley National Park below 2000 feet.


## How long the warning is in effect
The warning took effect Monday, October 5 at 3:32 p.m. PDT.

It remains in effect until Friday, October 9 at 8:00 p.m. PDT, the NWS said.


## Safety information
The National Weather Service advises that anyone overcome by heat should be moved to a cool and shaded location.
