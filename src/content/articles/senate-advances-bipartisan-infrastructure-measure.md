A late-night procedural vote in the United States Senate cleared the path Monday for final passage of a sweeping **$612 billion infrastructure package**, handing bipartisan negotiators a narrow but durable majority after weeks of behind-the-scenes wrangling over spending offsets.

The 67-31 vote limits debate on the compromise bill, which would direct tens of billions of dollars toward bridge repairs, rural broadband expansion, and hardening of the electric grid against extreme weather. Final passage is expected later this week.

## What the package includes

Negotiators said the measure concentrates roughly 40 percent of new spending on what they called "resilience" — projects designed to withstand floods, wildfires, and heat waves that have strained aging systems from the Gulf Coast to the Pacific Northwest.

- **$148 billion** for roads and bridges, with a priority list weighted toward structures rated structurally deficient.
- **$72 billion** for broadband, including a mandate that funded networks be capable of symmetrical gigabit speeds.
- **$61 billion** for grid modernization and transmission line expansion.
- **$34 billion** for water systems, with lead service line replacement accelerated.

## The fight over offsets

The bill's centrist architects insisted the spending would be largely offset by closing tax-code loopholes and repurposing unspent pandemic-era funds, a claim the nonpartisan budget referee is still reviewing.

> "This is the kind of durable, boring, bridge-and-wire work that Washington has promised for a generation and rarely delivered," said one senior negotiator. "Tonight we moved one step closer to actually delivering it."

Critics on both flanks objected. Some argued the package understates long-term maintenance costs, while others warned that the proposed offsets would not fully materialize. A final vote is expected before the end of the week, after which the measure heads to conference with the House.
