A NASA spacecraft cruising in the asteroid belt beyond Mars has transmitted the **sharpest close-range images ever captured** of a small, ancient rocky body that scientists believe has remained largely unchanged since the dawn of the solar system.

The images, received Friday at mission control, reveal a heavily cratered surface streaked with curious blue-gray veins — possible evidence of ancient water-driven geology on a world barely five miles across.

## Why this rock matters

Asteroids of this type are considered time capsules. Because they never grew large enough to melt and differentiate like planets, their surfaces preserve a chemical record from roughly 4.5 billion years ago.

- The **blue-gray veins** may point to minerals deposited by briny water long ago.
- **Boulders** scattered across one hemisphere suggest a relatively recent impact event.
- The body's **slow rotation** — once every 18 hours — gave cameras ample time to map it.

## What comes next

The spacecraft will continue circling the asteroid for another 14 months, using instruments to map its composition and gravity field. Scientists hope to determine whether the body is a fragment of a larger parent body or a primordial leftover.

> "We are looking at material that has been sitting in the dark, untouched, since before Earth existed," the mission's lead scientist said. "Every image is a page from the solar system's earliest chapter."

The findings will inform future sample-return missions and help refine models of how water and organic molecules were delivered to the inner planets.
