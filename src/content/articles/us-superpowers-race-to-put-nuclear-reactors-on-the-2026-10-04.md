Displaying these accomplishments graphically makes it easy to see how the U.S. started behind but eventually caught up and surpassed the Soviet Union. The U.S. was a year behind in getting a \"Man in Orbit\", and then jumped ahead during the Gemini Program. The Soviets did not accomplish rendezvous and docking until three years after the U.S. did.
In one of the most significant goals of the Space Race, the Soviets actually demonstrated the capability to beat the U.S. in sending people around the Moon, a fact which is rarely acknowledged.  Their Zond 5 spacecraft had the life support capability to get cosmonauts to the Moon safely.  But the capability that it lacked was a reliably survivable entry back into Earth's atmosphere.  The U.S. became aware of this threat that they could get beaten when in early 1968, satellite photography revealed the Proton rocket, which was calculated to have this lifting capacity to launch a human-rated spacecraft to the Moon.  This awareness was the catalyst which drove the decision to send Apollo 8 to lunar orbit.  This itself was an extremely risky decision.  But getting beaten by the Soviets yet again (after Sputnik, Gagarin, Tereshkova, Leonov, etc) was the last thing that NASA wanted to happen.  So the very first flight of the Saturn V with astronauts on board was sent all the way to the Moon.  This first mission of humans beyond Low Earth Orbit was a spectacular success.  Whereas the Soviets struggled with major disappointments.  Along with the Zond experiencing excessive g-forces upon entry, other major setbacks were followed by multiple failures of their N-1 Moon rocket and the Soviets never sent any cosmonauts beyond Earth orbit.  With six Apollo Moon landings, the U.S. resoundingly won the Space Race. The Soviets continued to explore the Moon with robotic probes.  Both nations continued sending probes to explore the solar system.  Through diplomatic negotiations, a new era of Detente was ushered in to significantly calm the fear of nuclear war.  After years of intense competition in space, the race was relaxed in 1975 with a dramatic display of cooperation on the Apollo-Soyuz mission.
