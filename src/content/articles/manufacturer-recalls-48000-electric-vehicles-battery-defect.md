An automaker is recalling roughly **48,000 electric vehicles** after federal safety regulators identified a battery cell defect that can, in rare cases, lead to thermal runaway and a fire risk, according to documents posted by the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration.

The recall covers certain 2023 through 2025 model-year vehicles equipped with a specific high-voltage battery pack. The agency said the issue stems from an inconsistent welding process at a supplier that can create an internal short circuit over time.

## What owners should do

The company said it will begin mailing notification letters next month and will replace the affected battery modules at no cost to owners. In the meantime, regulators offered interim guidance:

- Park outdoors and away from structures when possible until the repair is complete.
- Avoid charging the battery to 100 percent; limit to 80 percent if the vehicle allows.
- Contact the manufacturer's customer line or a local dealer to confirm whether your vehicle identification number is included.

## How the risk was found

The defect came to light after a small number of field reports described smoke and, in two cases, vehicle fires while parked. No injuries have been reported, according to the filing.

> "Battery thermal events are rare but serious, and the threshold for action here is appropriate," a safety advocate said. "The key question now is repair throughput — how quickly owners can actually get an appointment."

Owners can check their VIN through the manufacturer's website or the NHTSA recall database. The recall is expected to take several months to complete across the affected fleet.
