A team of researchers from three universities said this week it has identified a **single regulatory gene** that appears to help certain staple crops survive sustained heat waves, a discovery that could accelerate efforts to breed climate-resilient varieties.

The gene, described in a new paper, acts as a kind of master switch that coordinates a plant's response to heat stress, toggling protective pathways that shield cells from damage when temperatures spike.

## How the discovery was made

The researchers screened thousands of plant lines under controlled heat conditions in greenhouses, then validated the most promising candidates in field trials across two growing seasons.

- Plants with a **more active version** of the gene maintained photosynthesis during heat waves.
- Lines where the gene was **silenced** wilted faster and yielded less.
- The effect was consistent across **three related staple crops**, raising hopes for broad application.

## Why it matters

As heat waves grow more frequent and intense, breeders are racing to develop varieties that can maintain yields under stress. Traditionally that work has been slow and incremental; a single high-impact gene could shorten the timeline considerably.

> "We're not talking about engineering crops from scratch," the lead author said. "We're identifying a lever that already exists in the plant and giving breeders a faster way to use it."

The team is now working with agricultural institutions to incorporate the finding into commercial breeding pipelines. Wider field trials are expected to begin next planting season.
