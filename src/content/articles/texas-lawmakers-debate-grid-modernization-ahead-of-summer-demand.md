Texas lawmakers convened a special hearing this week on the future of the state's independent electric grid, weighing proposals for new transmission links and expanded battery storage as forecasts predict another summer of record-breaking demand.

The state's grid operator has repeatedly set all-time demand records in recent years, driven by population growth, electrification, and searing heat. While the system held through the most recent peak, officials warned that thinning reserve margins leave little room for error.

## The proposals on the table

Lawmakers are considering a slate of measures aimed at adding flexibility without fundamentally altering the state's go-it-alone approach to electricity:

- **Battery incentives** to fast-track large-scale storage projects that can discharge during evening peaks.
- **Strategic transmission** upgrades to move wind and solar power from the west to demand centers in the east.
- A limited **demand-response** expansion, paying large industrial users to curtail use during emergencies.

## The politics of interconnection

The most contentious debate is whether to allow even limited connections to neighboring grids. Proponents argue that a handful of tie-lines could import power during crises; critics contend it would erode the state's regulatory independence.

> "We don't need permission from Washington to keep the lights on," one state senator said. "But we do need to stop pretending the grid of 1999 will serve the Texas of 2030."

A working group is expected to issue recommendations before the next legislative session. In the meantime, the grid operator is finalizing its summer readiness plan.
