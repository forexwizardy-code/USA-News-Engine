NASA’s NISAR Earth-observing satellite tracked the spread of lava from the northern crater of Krasheninnikov, a volcano pair on Russia’s Kamchatka Peninsula, between December 2025 and August 2026. Researchers sequenced 17 radar frames captured through mid-August into a time-lapse video showing lava filling an inner caldera, overflowing into a wider crater, and widening into a fan.


## What JPL reported
According to NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory, the northern volcano began erupting a few days after an 8.8-magnitude earthquake struck the nearby ocean on July 30, 2025. The eruption was the first at Krasheninnikov in nearly five centuries.

JPL reports that NISAR captured its first image of Krasheninnikov on Dec. 25, 2025, just as the satellite was finishing post-launch checks and becoming operational. Since then, twice every 12 days, NISAR has returned to the same spot in orbit and taken detailed radar snapshots.


## What scientists observed
The time-lapse shows lava filling a smaller, inner caldera, then overflowing into a wider crater before widening into a fan. Lava appears lighter in the radar images because microwaves reflect more brightly from the new flow than from the surrounding snow or bare ground.

Matthew Pritchard, a geophysicist at Cornell University and NISAR science team member who analyzed the data, said the satellite’s consistent high-resolution observations every 12 days, captured in two observation directions, demonstrate its ability to closely monitor changing natural hazards.


## Mission or instrument context
NISAR is the first free-flying space mission to feature two radar instruments: an L-band system provided by NASA and an S-band system provided by ISRO, the Indian Space Research Organisation. The spacecraft’s drum-shaped reflector, 39 feet (12 meters) wide, is the largest radar antenna reflector NASA has launched into space, JPL says.

The mission is managed by Caltech for NASA; JPL leads the U.S. component and supplied the L-band SAR and antenna reflector. ISRO provided the spacecraft bus and its S-band SAR.


## What happens next
JPL says the data products from NISAR’s L-band radar are available at the Alaska Satellite Facility Distributed Active Archive Center in Fairbanks, which hosts and distributes all NASA synthetic aperture radar data.
