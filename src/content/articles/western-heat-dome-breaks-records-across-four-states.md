A sprawling **heat dome** parked over the Southwest shattered daily temperature records across four states this week, with desert valleys topping 115 degrees and urban centers opening emergency cooling centers around the clock.

The mass of stagnant, sinking air trapped heat near the ground and pushed the heat index in some low-elevation cities above 120 degrees, conditions forecasters described as dangerous even for acclimated residents.

## Record-setting heat

Preliminary data showed dozens of daily records falling across the region:

- **Phoenix** hit 116 degrees, breaking a mark set a decade ago.
- **Las Vegas** reached 114, with overnight lows barely dipping below 90.
- **Palm Springs** soared to 119, among the hottest spots in the country.
- **Tucson** and **Yuma** both set new September highs.

## Grid and health strain

The heat pushed electricity demand to seasonal highs as air conditioners ran nearly nonstop. Grid operators reported no major outages but urged conservation during the late afternoon peak.

Health officials warned that the most dangerous period is overnight, when temperatures that fail to cool rob the body of recovery time.

> "When it doesn't cool down at night, the risk accumulates day after day," a public health officer said. "Check on neighbors, especially older adults living alone."

Forecasters expect the dome to begin weakening by midweek as a weak front edges in from the Pacific.
