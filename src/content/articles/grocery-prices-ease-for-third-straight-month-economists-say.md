Grocery prices fell for a **third consecutive month**, government data released this week confirmed, giving consumers their longest sustained relief at the supermarket in roughly two years as supply chains normalize and competition intensifies among retailers.

The modest declines spanned a broad basket of staples, including eggs, bread, fresh produce, and cooking oils — categories that had driven much of the sticker shock of the past two years.

## Where prices moved

Not every aisle shared equally in the relief, but the breadth of the decline was notable:

- **Eggs** posted the steepest drop as flock recoveries outpaced seasonal demand.
- **Fresh produce** eased on improved growing conditions in the West.
- **Cooking oils** continued a months-long slide on stronger global harvests.
- **Beef** remained an outlier, ticking higher as herd rebuilding lags.

## Why it matters

Economists cautioned that grocery prices remain well above pre-pandemic levels, even with the recent retreat. But the direction of travel, they said, is meaningful for household budgets and for the broader inflation outlook.

> "Three months doesn't undo two years, but it's the first time in a long time that the trend is clearly working in shoppers' favor," one analyst said. "The question is whether it lasts into the holiday baking season."

Retailers are expected to lean into price competition through the fall, and several have already announced expanded private-label offerings. Analysts will be watching next month's report for signs the trend is holding.
