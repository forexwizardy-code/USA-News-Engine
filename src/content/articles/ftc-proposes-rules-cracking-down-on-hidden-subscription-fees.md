Federal regulators proposed a long-anticipated rule Friday that would force companies to display the full price of subscriptions upfront, make cancellation as easy as signing up, and send consumers annual reminders about recurring charges.

The Federal Trade Commission's draft rule targets what officials call "dark patterns" — design choices that steer users into recurring payments and then bury the off-ramp. The agency estimates Americans waste billions each year on subscriptions they no longer want.

## Core requirements

Under the proposal, sellers of recurring subscriptions would have to:

- Show the **total cost**, including all mandatory fees, before collecting payment information.
- Obtain **express, informed consent** before charging a discounted rate that rises after a trial.
- Provide a **simple cancellation mechanism** — no more phone-only cancel lines or buried menus.
- Send an **annual reminder** disclosing the charge and how to cancel.

## Industry pushback

Trade groups representing software and media companies warned the rule could discourage free trials and raise prices for honest businesses. The agency countered that the measure targets deception, not the subscription model itself.

> "If you can sign up with two taps, you should be able to cancel with two taps," a senior FTC official said. "Consumers are paying for subscriptions they forgot they even had."

The rule will be open for public comment for 60 days before a final version is adopted. Legal challenges from industry groups are widely expected once it is finalized.
